# Roadmap

Thin by design, per [project-principles](../design/project-principles.md): sequence and intent only. Each slice gets a
just-in-time plan in `docs/plans` when it starts (small enough to implement and verify in one session) and is archived
when implemented. Slice boundaries follow data availability; never more than one slice is planned in detail.

## Slices

0. **CLI skeleton — `leek`.** The first runnable surface: the `leek` entry point wired through `[project.scripts]`. A
   bare `leek` greets with an about card; `version` and `help` are the first verbs (ADR 0003). Data-backed commands
   arrive with the slices that have data behind them.

1. **Library core — `leek add`.** Music enters the database. The merged-view core tables and the source-layer write path
   (file tags as the only source, identity merge — layering from day one, machinery deferred). Tag reading via
   mediafile, copy-on-import, Alembic from the first migration. Makes TrackInfo/AlbumInfo real.

1. **Visibility — `leek list` and `leek info`.** The library becomes queryable and dogfoodable before anything clever.
   `info` exposes the source layer, not just the merged view.

1. **Matcher parity harness.** The riskiest work gets its verifier first: fixture inputs with beets' known matching
   outputs, which the port must reproduce. No porting in this slice.

1. **The matcher port and MusicBrainz — `leek match`.** The second source arrives, and with it the entities its data
   populates (release groups, recordings, works) and recorded matches with confidence. Ported against the harness, never
   ahead of it.

1. **Merge machinery and review — `leek review`.** With two sources real, merging earns its keep: merge strategies, the
   pending-changes queue, accept/reject, auto-accept rules.

## Later

Not planned in detail; build when needed. `leek organize` (tag writing, path templates), `leek import` (the full
pipeline), further sources (Discogs, RED), background re-polling, the beets migration tool, attachments, format variants
and relationships, listening history.
